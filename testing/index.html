MPD Tester Handbook

Every scenario a person can check by hand in the running app, in a sensible order, for one tester working alone. Each scenario says who you are, what to do, and exactly what you should see. Record the result as you go: it is saved in this browser, and you can download it. Open testing/index.html from the folder; nothing needs the internet.

1. How to use this handbook

0 scenarios in total. Smoke scenarios (a quick check that the whole app is alive) are marked smoke.

  1. Do Setup (section 2) once. It starts the app and builds the small "test world" that every later scenario uses.
  2. Work through the areas in the order shown on the left. Later areas rely on data made by earlier ones. Press Next scenario to do to jump to your place.
  3. For each scenario press Passed, Failed, Blocked (you could not get to it, say why) or Skipped. Add a short note. Press the same button again to clear it.
  4. When something fails, write the bug report (section 5) and carry on. A failed scenario does not stop the next one unless it says so.
  5. At the end, download the results (CSV or JSON) and send them with your bug list.
Kinds of scenario: core the main thing the feature is for. edge a boundary or unusual case. security someone who must be refused: these matter as much as the happy paths, because they protect money and private data. smoke a short pass to run before anything else after every update.
This handbook grows with the product. Every update to MPD adds the scenarios a tester needs for it (rule: CLAUDE.md and mpd-context/08-workflow.md). The version line at the bottom of this page says which update it is current for.

2. Setup (do this once, in this order)

2.1 What you need

  • The developer's machine or your own with Git, Node 22, pnpm and Docker Desktop (see workspace/NEW-MACHINE.md).
  • Chrome for most testing, plus two more isolated browser sessions (a second Chrome profile and a private window, or Firefox). The app keeps your sign-in in cookies per browser profile, so one profile can be signed in as only one person at a time. Scenarios need two or three people at once (for example a customer and a worker).
  • A phone, or Chrome's phone view (F12, then the device icon) for the phone scenarios.
  • A notes file for bug reports, and (optionally) a screen recorder.

2.3 Where the emails are

No real email is sent yet. Every email (verification codes, staff invitations, owner invitations, notifications) is printed in the terminal that runs the API (terminal 3) and by the worker (terminal 4), as to=... subject="..." followed by the text. Verification codes (6 digits) and invitation links (.../admin/accept-invite?token=..., .../owner-invite?token=...) are found there. Copy them from the terminal. Notification emails are printed by the worker within a minute.

The one rule behind everything: boxes first, then people

An organisation is created by someone above it. A person is invited into an organisation; nobody can be invited into a box that does not exist yet. So the order is always: create the box, then invite the person into it.

How to create an organisation (a box)

  1. Open Organisations. You see the tree, starting with MPD.
  2. On the row of the parent press the + icon (its tooltip says "Add under ...").
  3. Type the Name, choose the Type (only the types that fit under that parent are offered) and press save. A master franchise belongs to one city, and only one master franchise per city is allowed.
  4. Give it an area: press the map icon (Territories) on the row, Add a territory, type a name and the pincodes it holds. A franchise's pincodes must lie inside its master franchise's and may not overlap another franchise. A provider chooses from its franchise's pincodes and may overlap other providers.

How to create a person (an account for staff)

  1. Open Staff and press Invite.
  2. Choose the organisation (the box), the role (only roles that fit that box and are below you are offered), and type the person's name and email. Use an address you invent, such as ops1@example.com.
  3. Press Send invitation. The person appears under Waiting to join.
  4. There is no real email yet. Find the invitation in the terminal that runs the API: look for to=ops1@example.com and copy the link that looks like .../admin/accept-invite?token=....
  5. Open that link in another browser profile or a private window (so you stay signed in as the Super admin), choose a password of at least 10 characters, and sign in at http://localhost:3000/admin/login.
  6. The link works once. If it is lost or used, press Invite again.

If the email already has an account, the person is simply given the extra role (no new password).

How to create a customer

You do not. A customer opens http://localhost:3000/signup, enters name, email and password, and types the 6-digit code that is printed in the API terminal (the same to=... line). Then they sign in at /login. A customer who forgets the password uses Forgot password on the login page; the reset link also appears in the API terminal. A project owner (builder or society) signs up the same way and Operations switch the customer type (see the customer scenarios).

How to choose what a service costs and how it is charged (sq ft, lump sum, per hour)

Every service has one rate type that says what the price means, and a price for each pincode. Three rate types exist from the start: Lump sum (one price for the whole service), Per sq ft (the price is multiplied by the property's built area) and Per hour. Services that were lump-sum in the old system, such as the site visit, start as Lump sum; the others start with no rate type and cannot be bought until you choose one.

  1. Open Pricing. On the Price lists tab press New price list and name it; a list is a Draft until you Publish it (you give a start date), and prices can only be typed in a draft.
  2. Open the Service prices tab, choose your draft at the top, find the service (use the search) and press its row to open the price window. The row shows its current rate type, or "Not set".
  3. If the service has no rate type, the window asks How is this service charged?: pick Lump sum, Per sq ft or Per hour.
  4. To change it later (for example Site visit from Lump sum to Per sq ft), use Change how it is charged at the top of the same window and pick the other rate type. This is allowed only while the service has no prices in any price list: the same number would mean something different. If you already typed prices, remove them first (the bin icon on each pincode row, and clear the default), save, then change the rate type.
  5. Type the Default price (used in every pincode without its own price), an optional Minimum charge, and optional prices for single pincodes. Save, then Publish the list when it is ready.
  6. Need a unit that is not there (per sq yd, per visit hour)? Open the Rate types tab, press New rate type, choose how it is counted and the unit, and it appears in the choices above. A rate type that is in use can be switched off but never deleted.

What the Super admin can do that Operations cannot

Very little, on purpose. Operations run the day-to-day business and nearly every screen and action is open to both. The differences are about who controls the accounts at the top:

Super adminOperations
Everything day-to-day (customers, orders, payments, prices, catalogue, legal, reports, payouts, content)YesYes
Create organisations, invite master franchises and everyone belowYesYes
Invite or create another Super adminYesNo (the option is missing or refused)
Suspend, reactivate or change a Super adminYes, any other one (never yourself)No
Suspend or reactivate an Operations personYesNo: a person can only manage people ranked below them, and Operations are equals (Operations can still invite another Operations person)
Approve a payment they recorded themselvesNoNo (the person who records a payment can never approve it; a second person must)

So the Super admin is the owner of the keys: only that role can create or remove the other top-level accounts. Keep one Super admin for yourself and create separate Operations accounts (OPS1 and OPS2) for the work, because the two-person rule for payments and payouts needs two different people.

Starting again: stop the API, worker and web app, then run pnpm db:fresh in mpd-backend. It deletes the development database, builds it again and loads only the reference lists, the service catalogue, the legal forms, the website blocks and the one Super admin. The automated tests never touch this database: they use their own (mpd_test) and delete everything they create.

2.6 Making time pass

Several scenarios involve waiting (reminders, acceptance limits, plan expiry). You cannot wait days, so each scenario tells you how to set the data so the condition is true now (for example a visit time within 24 hours, a document expiry in 5 days), then wait up to 10 minutes for the worker's sweep. Where a developer is needed to change a date, the scenario says so and offers the SQL or the API call.

2.7 Resetting

To start again from nothing: stop the API, the worker and the web app, then run pnpm db:fresh in mpd-backend (it deletes the development database and rebuilds it with only the reference lists, the service catalogue, the legal forms, the website blocks and the one Super admin; see 2.4a), then start the servers again as in 2.2. Your recorded results stay in this browser until you press Reset results.

4. Ground rules that apply to every scenario

  • Never use real people's data. Names, emails, phone numbers and documents must be invented. Use harmless sample files (a small PDF or photo you made).
  • Money is whole paise. Rs 1,000 is shown as "₹1,000". Check totals by hand.
  • Two people for money. The person who records a payment or a payout can never approve it. Always have a second account ready.
  • What you see depends on your role. If a menu item is missing for a role, that is usually correct. The scenarios say when it is not.
  • Refusals are results. "You do not have permission", "not found" and a clear message are the expected outcome of the security scenarios.
  • Write the exact text of any error message in your notes.

5. Writing a bug report

Title:     one line, what went wrong (not what you think the cause is)
Scenario:  the ID, for example JOB-07
Who/where: the account you were signed in as, the page address, the browser
Steps:     numbered, exactly what you did, with the data you typed
Expected:  what the scenario says should happen
Actual:    what happened, with the exact message text
Evidence:  a screenshot, and the lines the API terminal printed at that moment
How bad:   Blocks the work / Wrong result / Looks wrong / Wrong wording

Always include the API terminal output around the time of the failure: it names the exact error.
